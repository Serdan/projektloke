# Methodology

Projekt Loke documents patterns of organized bigotry in Denmark, with primary emphasis on transphobia.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Primary records: Folketinget, official documents, court material, party publications, organizational statements, original articles and recordings.
2. Evidence syntheses and high-quality secondary research used to assess underlying empirical claims.
3. Secondary reporting used to establish context or locate primary material.
4. Commentary and social media used as evidence of rhetoric, interpretation or transmission only when attribution is clear.

The hierarchy is not a single credibility score. A primary political document is strongest for what the institution said or did, but may be poor evidence for an empirical claim. A systematic review may be strong evidence synthesis while being irrelevant to a particular legal or political claim.

## Claim discipline

Each substantive claim should be classifiable as one of:

- documented fact;
- synthesis from multiple documented facts;
- interpretation;
- open question.

Do not turn contact into coordination, chronological sequence into causation, or rhetorical similarity into proof of direct borrowing without evidence for that stronger claim.

## Sources, materials and claim support

A source records provenance. A material node represents a report, guideline, judgment, plan or other document that itself becomes a recurring object of analysis. Criticism, rebuttal, professional responses, media coverage and political uptake should attach to the material actually being discussed rather than being flattened into one relation type.

Materials and direct material links record separate source-quality dimensions: independence, peer-review status, methodological strength and evidence role. Evidentiary relevance is evaluated separately for the specific relation or claim. High-risk or contested assertions may additionally use stable claim assessments with their own provenance and a claim-specific methodological-strength override.

Evidence conflicts should be recorded only where assessments actually overlap. Distinguish direct contradiction from partial overlap or disagreement about interpretation/policy under shared uncertainty. Conflict records do not imply equal evidentiary weight.

## Sampling and denominators

Examples can establish existence, transmission and chronology; they cannot by themselves establish prevalence. Claims about media, rhetoric, organizations or institutions require denominators or a documented sampling frame where possible. Actor trails, politically selected media sources and structured media samples must remain separate corpus roles.

## Publication boundary

Working notes can be messy, speculative and incomplete. Public site material cannot. Material moves from research into the site only after the relevant claims, source links and important counterevidence have been checked.

## Corrections

Material corrections should be documented rather than silently rewritten when the original error was already public or materially affected an interpretation.
