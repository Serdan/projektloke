# Coverage review — 11 September 2026

## Bottom line

Projekt Loke is now broad enough to function as a real evidence base rather than a collection of examples. The main limitation is no longer total volume, but uneven maturity between domains.

Current public-data scale is approximately:

- 176 sources, about 140 explicitly primary-source-type records
- 39 actors
- 53 contextualised statements
- 48 events
- 24 healthcare records
- 15 documented relationships
- 11 media records
- 7 first-class material nodes
- 11 material links
- 63 generated pages

## Coverage by area

### Politics — strong

Probably the strongest part of the project. B62 → B12 → B47 → B145 is well documented, with B80/B72 and the 2023 legal-gender administrative change providing history and context. Voting, rhetoric, actors and source chains are integrated.

The project can now support a nuanced central finding: a recognisable restrictive transpolitical agenda has acquired sustained parliamentary reach, moved across multiple substantive domains, and has identifiable organisational, media and international transmission paths — but it has not become a uniform Danish institutional or governmental position.

The longitudinal coverage is already adequate for the present purpose. Events reach back to 2016, statements to 2021, and the political prehistory explicitly includes the 2014 legal-gender framework and B80 in 2020. Finding older examples should therefore be lower priority unless they establish an important genealogy.

### Healthcare — strong

Healthcare has become a second major pillar. The project now follows a useful chain from national rules → newer evidence → professional consultation → local implementation → capacity/access → parliamentary response.

The Cass/York/BMA material substantially improves this area because the project can represent the final Cass report, commissioned evidence, criticism, rebuttal and later reanalysis as distinct objects rather than flattening them into a binary “Cass good/Cass bad” frame.

The largest healthcare gap is current operational data. Important unresolved questions include:

- current youth referral → assessment → treatment throughput;
- actual contemporary blocker/hormone initiation rates;
- current rejection/closure rates and reasons;
- real waiting-time distributions rather than isolated published figures;
- outcomes for the 16 reopened minor legal-gender cases and subsequent applicants.

Good administrative datasets would now add more than simply accumulating further academic commentary.

### Legal and institutional cases — moderate to strong

Minor legal gender, the Supreme Court prison judgment, changing-room/legal outcomes, education and football provide genuinely different institutional contexts rather than repetitions of one dispute.

Coverage is still case-based, so broad claims about all Danish institutions would be premature, but the existing cases are individually well documented.

### Rhetoric — moderate to strong

The 53 statements are enough to demonstrate recurring rhetorical forms, but the corpus remains selection-biased. Dansk Folkeparti accounts for a large share of the recorded statements, and much of the material originates in already-identified political cases.

The current dataset answers “which recurring arguments have we documented?” much better than “how prevalent are these arguments in Danish political discourse?” The public methodology correctly preserves that distinction.

The next major improvement should be systematic coding of complete debates, including opposing speakers, rather than simply collecting more striking quotations. Full-debate coding would introduce denominators and reduce selection effects.

### Actors — moderate to strong

The 39 actors provide useful coverage of the parliamentary ecosystem and several organisations/media figures. Political actors are much better represented than the wider civil-society and media ecosystem.

### Networks and transmission — moderate

The strongest network links are persuasive because they use different evidence types: direct organisation→parliament transmission, repeated Folkemødet infrastructure, international relationships and media→politics transmission.

However, 15 relationships are still too few for broad structural claims about a unified anti-trans network.

The current depth around Dansk Regnbueråd creates a risk that the organisation appears synonymous with the wider ecosystem simply because it has been researched most deeply. Future network work should deliberately expand outward to other organisations, recurring experts/commentators, parties, media, international organisations, conferences/events and funding relationships.

The aim should be to discover whether Dansk Regnbueråd is central, peripheral, one cluster among several, or simply unusually transparent — not to assume coordination in advance.

### Media — weakest major area

Media remains the clearest bottleneck.

There are 11 media records, but only 3 belong to the structured sample. Four are actor-trail records and four are political-source records. This is enough to document individual frames, platforming, specific media→politics transmission, and rapid Danish uptake of Cass. It is not enough for general statements about Danish journalism.

At present the project can responsibly say that:

- a particular frame appeared;
- a particular journalist or outlet platformed it;
- a particular media story was subsequently cited in Parliament;
- Cass was rapidly transmitted into Danish coverage;
- some coverage compressed evidentiary uncertainty into stronger treatment conclusions.

It cannot yet responsibly conclude that:

- Danish media generally frame trans issues in a particular way;
- one outlet is systematically more hostile than another;
- trans voices are systematically excluded across Danish journalism;
- Cass coverage nationally was predominantly restrictive.

Closing the remaining Cass archive gaps is useful, but the next major gain should come from two or three additional event-based structured samples rather than indefinitely enlarging the Cass sample. Good candidates would cover different substantive domains, such as legal gender, sport and a domestic healthcare/political event.

### Quantitative hostile discourse — useful but narrow

The TrygFonden / Analyse & Tal Facebook data provides a valuable independent quantitative baseline. Its limitation is scope: one platform/dataset ending in June 2024, with a broad “kønsminoriteter” category rather than trans people alone.

### Countervailing evidence — improving but still thinner than hostile/restrictive material

The 2026 government, LGBT+ action plan, inclusive parliamentary statements and all-party survey prevent the site from becoming an opposition-only archive.

This should continue to expand. Counterevidence is methodologically important because it allows the project to establish where restrictive agendas have not prevailed and where institutions or parties actively resist them.

The party-position survey is close to publishable as a multidimensional matrix, provided it keeps separate columns for current/formal party text, parliamentary behaviour and source date. It should not collapse those dimensions into a single pro/anti-trans score.

### Methodology and source criticism — very strong

The evidence architecture is now unusually mature relative to corpus size:

- first-class material nodes;
- explicit independence, peer-review status, methodological strength and evidence role;
- evidentiary relevance separate from source quality;
- claim-level support assessments;
- claim provenance;
- explicit evidence-conflict records;
- independent generated-output verification.

The methodological layer is currently ahead of corpus breadth, which is preferable to the reverse.

## Under-covered substantive domains

Several domains currently appear only weakly or not as coherent case studies:

- employment and workplace discrimination;
- family and parenthood law;
- municipal/local-government policy;
- prison policy beyond the Supreme Court case;
- identity documents beyond CPR/legal gender;
- everyday implementation of anti-discrimination protections.

These should not all be opened simultaneously. Add them when a documented controversy, legal decision or policy change provides a natural case, rather than trading depth for breadth.

## Overall maturity assessment

- Evidence architecture: **mature**
- Politics: **mature**
- Healthcare: **approaching mature**, pending operational/administrative data
- Legal/institutional coverage: **solid but case-based**
- Rhetoric: **substantial but non-representative**
- Networks: **credible early map**
- Media: **pilot-stage**

The project should no longer optimize primarily for raw record count. The highest-value additions now are denominators, structured sampling, countervailing observations and administrative datasets.

## Recommended next workstreams

1. **Expand structured media sampling.** Close the remaining Cass archive gaps, then add two or three event-based samples across different issue domains using the same outlet set and coding protocol.
2. **Pursue youth-treatment and minor legal-gender administrative data.** Capacity, throughput, outcomes and waiting-time distributions would materially strengthen the healthcare findings.
3. **Publish the all-party position matrix.** Preserve the distinction between formal/current text, parliamentary behaviour and source date, and avoid a one-dimensional ideological score.

These three would improve inferential strength more than simply increasing the number of examples already represented well in the corpus.
## Follow-up — 11 September 2026

The first recommendation has now been addressed structurally. Structured media observations belong to explicit sampling-frame records rather than carrying `struktureret prøve` as an ungrounded label. The Cass pilot exposes its fixed 9 April–10 May 2024 window, all seven target outlets and per-outlet verification state: three verified observations, two partial archive trails and two not verified in the available open sources. Null findings remain search-coverage metadata, not evidence of no coverage.

Two additional cross-domain media candidates were probed: Marie Bjerre's August 2024 legal-gender intervention and DBU Sjælland's September 2025 tournament rules. Both have strong event baselines and downstream reaction, but neither currently has sufficiently uniform seven-outlet archive coverage for public promotion. This means the immediate media constraint is now archive/full-text access rather than an underspecified sampling method.

The administrative-data sweep also reconfirmed a useful denominator: Region Hovedstaden reported that 239 young people started puberty blockers or gender-affirming hormones during 2016–2023, while stating that precise annual treatment-start counts could not be produced. That aggregate is now public on the health page with the limitation attached. Current KKBU material continues to describe medical treatment as formally available through Vækst og Reproduktion, but the project still lacks comparable annual post-reorganisation data for referral, waiting time, treatment recommendation and treatment start.

### Updated priority implication

1. Keep the media sampling frame and continue archive work, but do not spend open-web search effort pretending it can substitute for uniform archive access.
2. Administrative youth-treatment data are now the highest-value unresolved denominator because public sources have reached their limit.
3. The all-party matrix remains publication-ready in concept and is the strongest workstream that does not depend on external data access.
